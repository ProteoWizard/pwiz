CarafeSharp library workflow

Top-to-bottom data flow from a protein FASTA and DIA runs to the fine-tuned BiblioSpecLite (.blib) library Osprey searches with, drawn around the six stages of Run-CarafeSharpWorkflow.ps1 (Mike MacCoss's Carafe "Workflow 5"), which are also the modes of the CarafeSharp command line. Two of the six stages are Osprey's: CarafeSharp reads no spectra. Osprey searches the training run and exports its per-ion evidence (<stem>.training.parquet), and CarafeSharp applies Carafe's masking rules to that evidence instead of matching peaks itself. Every stage's products are named in the banner heading it, so a stage can be rerun alone (-Stages) from the products of the one before, which is also the checkpoint contract the stages keep when they become Osprey tasks in the integration sprint. CarafeSharp is a cross-implementation port of Carafe 2.2.0 (maccoss/Carafe): box color shows where it reproduces Carafe, where it differs by design, and what it adds. The comparison numbers are in the tables at the bottom.

Against Carafe 2.2.0, stage by stage (one end-to-end run per tool and dataset, 2026-09-28 and 29, Stellar HeLa _21 and Astral HeLa _55 as the training runs, the same Osprey build searching both arms; the full account is docs/05-carafe-comparison.md):

StageResultWhy
1 DigestsDiffer by designThe similarity gate from maccoss/Carafe main, which the 2.2.0 build predates; with --no_similarity_gate the FASTAs are byte-identical.
2 Initial libraryThe same libraryFragment lists identical on the shared precursors, cosine over 0.99999998; the precursor sets differ only by the redrawn decoys.
3 Search of the training runNearly the same IDsTotals within 0.6%; precursor Jaccard 0.977 (Stellar) and 0.896 (Astral); the IDs one arm has sit near the 1% threshold.
4 Training set85.7-85.9% slot agreementMasking from Osprey's evidence rather than Carafe's own peak matching (docs/02-masking.md).
5 Fine-tuned libraryTwo different fine-tunesMedian sampled cosine 0.998-0.999, median RT difference 0.016-0.017 min; about one fewer weak fragment per precursor.
TimeCarafeSharp 1.34x (Stellar) and 1.50x (Astral) faster end to endNo msconvert, and no second pass over the spectra.

Library step (docs/03-performance.md, GeForce GTX 1650, CUDA build, medians of interleaved runs): Stellar 967,226 precursors in 179 s, Astral 6,170,973 in 1,054 s, 1.36x faster since the writer thread; prediction is the whole cost and writing is hidden. The step is deterministic on the GPU: two runs of one build give identical tables.

What CarafeSharp adds over Carafe (blue boxes): Chronologer as the default RT model and its fine-tuning (docs/07-chronologer.md); run alignment onto the hydrophobic index so runs of different gradients train one model; the saved .model.zip that predicts any peptides later without training again (docs/06-saved-models.md); the .blib writer with peak annotations and whole decoy pairs; and training on Osprey's exports named directly, with no spectra read. What it leaves to Osprey (yellow): peak matching, XICs and boundaries, which Carafe computes itself and CarafeSharp takes from the training export.

Where the stages are going. In the Osprey-CarafeSharp integration sprint (TODO-20261014_osprey_carafe_integration.md in pwiz-ai) each stage becomes a task on the pipeline contract Osprey's tasks use, with the files in its banner as its checkpoint, and osprey --fasta proteome.fasta --input run1 run2 run3 runs all six in one process with resume. CarafeSharp.exe stays as the library-only front end that accepts Carafe's command lines. This page then folds into Osprey-workflow.html as its library-build chapter.
